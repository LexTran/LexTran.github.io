AIGC efficiency exploration

The goalReduce the cost of generation while monitoring changes in image detail and video appearance.

My workExplored acceleration approaches, compared generated outputs, and organized the findings for internal knowledge sharing.

Wan2.1 T2V · Combining acceleration methods

On a single NVIDIA L20, the experiment progressively added SageAttention, FP8 with torch.compile, TeaCache, and SpargeAttention. The recorded DiT-stage time decreased from 1120 s to 328 s, with a reported 3.41× speedup. The columns show cumulative additions; every speedup is relative to the BF16 baseline.

TeaCache · Cache reuse and visual quality

Explored how different TeaCache thresholds affect speed and visual output. TeaCache estimates changes between diffusion steps to decide when cached outputs can be reused. The examples below compare a photograph-style scene and an illustration at three acceleration levels. Method paper ↗

Scroll horizontally to compare all settings →

PipeFusion · Exploring multi-GPU inference

Compared a single GPU with two-GPU sequence parallelism (SP) and two-GPU PipeFusion. PipeFusion uses patch-level pipeline parallelism to coordinate computation and communication. In this experiment, two-GPU PipeFusion reduced the recorded time from 22.95 s to 13.51 s, a 1.70× speedup over the single-GPU baseline. Method paper ↗

Scroll horizontally to compare all settings →

Sampling optimization · Region-Adaptive Sampling

Explored Region-Adaptive Sampling (RAS), which allocates updates across image regions and reuses cached noise elsewhere. The two settings recorded in the experiment reduced time from 26.9 s to 23.1 s and 18.8 s. The examples compare scene composition and the rendering of text. Method paper ↗

RAS speedups are calculated from the recorded times: 26.9 ÷ 23.1 ≈ 1.16×; 26.9 ÷ 18.8 ≈ 1.43×. The labels 0.75 and 0.50 follow the experiment settings in the slides.
